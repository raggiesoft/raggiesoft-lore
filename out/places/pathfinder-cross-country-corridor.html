The Pathfinder Exodus (Norfolk to Seattle)

The Pathfinder Exodus (The Virginia to Washington Route)

This is a shared piece of universal lore across the RaggieSoft universe. Whenever a narrative features a disabled protagonist being dragged across the country by their abuser from Virginia to Washington State, they are subjected to this exact grueling, multi-day Pathfinder bus route.

The Narrative Motif: It is crucial to understand that these protagonists are not on the same bus together. This is a recurring traumatic motif that happens across entirely different timelines. For example, Leo is dragged across the country by his abuser in 2003, while Michael endures this exact route with Delores in November 2006.

The Logistics: The entire trip takes exactly four days (96 brutal hours) of constant forward motion. Because it is the absolute cheapest way to cross the continent, the tickets cost a mere $99 per person—a cruel reflection of how little financial investment the abuser is willing to put into the protagonist's comfort or safety.

It is not just a geographic route; it is a psychological descent. The trip strips the protagonist of their agency, inflicts severe physical damage (due to lack of medical accommodation), and isolates them completely from their support systems.

The Official Routing and Narrative Waypoints

Leg 1: The Virginia Departure

The initial shock of leaving the East Coast.

  • Norfolk, VA: (Departure City). The journey begins. The initial shock of leaving the East Coast. Depending on the protagonist, the starting point varies, but the route always converges.
  • Norfolk, VA: (Departure City for Michael). The journey begins.
  • Richmond, VA: (Bus Change).
  • Charlottesville, VA: (Stop).
  • Staunton, VA: (Restroom/Rest Stop).
  • Roanoke, VA: (Restroom/Rest Stop).
  • Roanoke, VA (Campbell Court): (Departure City for Freddy Avery from the /paige narrative). For others, it serves as a Restroom/Rest Stop.
  • Wytheville, VA: (Implied Stop, preparing to hit the border).
  • Marion, VA: (Major Stop). Crucial Routing Note: The bus stops here specifically to service the massive Federal Trade Corps facility . It acts as a major loading/unloading point for young adults traveling into or out of the federal training program.
  • Bristol, VA/TN: The terminal sits precisely on the Virginia side of the border. Crossing into Tennessee marks the definitive exit from the protagonist's home state.

Leg 2: Tennessee to the Midwest (The Point of No Return)

The grueling reality of cross-country transit sets in.

  • Knoxville, TN: (Bus Change).
  • Nashville, TN: Narrative Event: The psychological breaking point for the protagonist, where they stare out the window and desperately wish they could turn around and go back.
  • St. Louis, MO (Downtown Terminal): (Bus Change).
  • St. Louis, MO (Lambert Airport - STL): Narrative Event (The Overhead Rack Incident): Shortly after departing downtown for the airport stop, improperly stowed wheelchair leg rests dislodge from the overhead storage rack and come crashing down directly onto the abuser's head. The Pathfinder driver is forced to pull the bus over to check on her. Though she is left crying from the physical impact, her pride and stubbornness force her to insist the trip continue.
  • Kansas City, MO/KS: (Stop).
  • Topeka, KS: (Stop).
  • Colby, KS: Narrative Event: A brief, desolate layover at a truck stop. This is a critical psychological waypoint where the exhaustion finally hits the abuser. For a fleeting, bitter moment, the abuser regrets the trip and wishes she could turn around, but her pride forces them forward.

Leg 3: The Rocky Mountain Bypass

  • Denver, CO: (Bus Change).
  • Fort Collins, CO: The bus exits Interstate 25.
  • US Highway 287 (The Cutoff): Crucial Routing Note: The Pathfinder driver explicitly avoids taking I-70 through the treacherous, high-altitude Eisenhower Tunnel. Instead, the bus runs up US-287 from Fort Collins directly into Wyoming.
  • Laramie, WY: The bus reconnects with Interstate 80 here, having successfully executed the flatter, safer commercial transit bypass around the harshest peaks of the Rockies.

Leg 4: The Great Basin & The Pacific Northwest (The Final Decline)

The final stretch, where physical and emotional abuse reach their climax.

  • Salt Lake City, UT: (Bus Change). Narrative Event (The Pressure Sore): A devastating physical realization. This is the exact layover where the wheelchair-using protagonist discovers the beginning of a severe pressure sore. Sitting in the rigid, unyielding bus seats for days without their proper medical equipment or weight-shifting relief has begun to break down their skin.
  • Boise, ID: (Brief Stop).
  • Pasco, WA: (Restroom Stop). Narrative Event (The Assault): The physical abuse boils over. Exhausted, angry, and near the end of the line, the abuser assaults the protagonist in or around the terminal restrooms.
  • Pasco, WA (Pasco Intermodal Terminal): (Scheduled Rest Stop). Narrative Event (The Near-Assault): A profoundly terrifying escalation. Inside the isolation of the terminal restrooms, the abuser attempts to sexually assault the protagonist. The only thing that stops the horrific act from fully occurring is the strict, ticking clock of the commercial transit schedule—the abuser abruptly stops only because she realizes they are running out of time before the Pathfinder bus pulls away. It is a chilling, suffocating moment of boundary violation, halted solely by cold external logistics rather than any shred of morality.
  • Yakima, WA: (Stop).
  • Ellensburg, WA: (Stop).
  • Seattle, WA: The final arrival at the massive Pathfinder terminal near King Street Station. The protagonist is dumped in the Pacific Northwest—physically injured, traumatized, and completely isolated from everyone they know.

Lore Dossier: Chicago Transit

This document maps the real-world Chicago transit networks and fare media to their in-universe Raggiesoft equivalents.

Transit Agencies

  • CMA (Chicago Metropolitan Authority): The in-universe counterpart to the CTA (Chicago Transit Authority). Operates the city bus grid and the iconic elevated and subway trains. Instead of the "L", locals refer to the train system simply as "The Loop" or "The Elevated".
  • Heartland Commuter: The in-universe counterpart to Metra. Operates the extensive, multi-line commuter rail network connecting the Chicago downtown terminal stations (like Union Station) to the outer suburbs.
  • Stride Transit: The in-universe counterpart to Pace. Operates the suburban bus network that feeds into the CMA and Heartland Commuter systems.

Fare Media & History

  • 2003 Era (CityCard): During the events of Luna and Leo (Summer 2003), transit riders use the CityCard (the equivalent to the real-life magnetic stripe Transit Cards and the early RFID Chicago Card). Stella has to buy paper CityCards from the automated vending machines, which get sucked in and popped back out by the turnstiles.

  • Modern Era (OmniPass): The in-universe counterpart to Ventra. Introduced roughly a decade later (matching the 2013-2014 Ventra debut), the OmniPass is a unified, tap-to-pay contactless smart card that works seamlessly across CMA, Heartland Commuter, and Stride Transit.

  • Lakeshore Interurban: The in-universe counterpart to the South Shore Line (NICTD). This electric commuter rail line operates out of the downtown Chicago stations and runs east, hugging the southern coast of Lake Michigan to connect Chicago with the Indiana suburbs and cities (like Hammond, Gary, and South Bend).
