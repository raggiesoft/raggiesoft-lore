Gazetteer & Worldbuilding Notes

Ocean State Transit Authority (OSTA) - East Bay Transit Spine

This document outlines the core transit spine bridging the "Wickford Void" and connecting Braxton, Newport, and Providence via OSTA (Ocean State Transit Authority).

The Hub: Jamestown Bridge Park & Ride

Location: I-895 at Route 1A (Corresponds to the real-life Route 138 at Route 1A interchange / North Kingstown Park & Ride). Function: The critical transfer point and border checkpoint where the state's regional routes converge.

OSTA Core Routes

OSTA Route 14: The Braxton Express

  • Route: Braxton Central Terminal ↔ Jamestown Bridge Park & Ride ↔ PVD Airport ↔ Kennedy Plaza (Providence).
  • Service Pattern: Operates Express from Braxton Central Terminal to the Jamestown Bridge Park & Ride. After the Park & Ride, it operates Local straight up the US-1 corridor to PVD Airport and Providence Kennedy Plaza.

OSTA Route 12: The Newport/Providence Link

  • Route: Newport Gateway Center ↔ Jamestown Bridge Park & Ride ↔ PVD Airport ↔ Kennedy Plaza (Providence).
  • Service Pattern: Operates Express out of Newport, crossing both the Newport Pell Bridge and the Jamestown Bridge (I-895 "The Waiver") to reach the Jamestown Bridge Park & Ride. After the Park & Ride, it turns North and operates Local, serving the exact same stops as Route 14 up the US-1 corridor to PVD and Providence.

OSTA Route 64: The URI / Kingston Connector

  • Route: A direct 1:1 functional equivalent of the real-life RIPTA Route 64, with the vital addition of servicing the Jamestown Bridge Park & Ride. Connects Newport to Rhode Island State University and Kingston Station.

Braxton: World Bible

  1. Overview & Geography • The Valley & The Point (The Public): The city center and the public university sit on a flat, expansive peninsula jutting into the Narraganset Bay. This area is modern, accessible, and integrated with transit. • The Hill (The Elite): To the North, a steep ridge known as Mount Regalis (locally "The Knob") looms over the city. This is where the old money lives, physically looking down on the rest of the population. • The South Shore (The Retreat): Far to the south, separated by the river delta and industrial zones, are the quiet, spacious coastal suburbs where the Fortress resides.

  2. The Universities: "Built vs. Bought" Braxton State University (BSU) • Nickname: "The Bulls." • Location: Beacon Point Peninsula. A massive, modern campus at the water's edge. • Motto: Ex Terra Vires (Strength from the Earth). • Identity: Public, diverse, gritty, and universally accessible. • The Campus: A "Universal Design" paradise. Automatic doors, ramps integrated into architecture, and the car-free Esplanade waterfront park. King’s College • Nickname: "The Royals." • Rival Name for BSU: "Bull S**t U" or "The Barn." • Location: The summit of Mount Regalis. • Motto: Corona Non Cadit (The Crown Does Not Fall). • Identity: Private, snobby, pre-Revolutionary (founded 1755). • Athletics: Known for "The King’s Fellowship"—a legal loophole used to "buy" star athletes with massive endowments. They send scouts ("King's Men") to high schools to recruit talent. • Access: A gated fortress of privilege, accessible primarily by luxury cars.

  3. The Braxton Transit Authority (BTA) Nickname: "Better Take Alternatives" or "Broken Train Again." The maps are logical, the system is extensive, and it is his path to independence. A. The Metro (Subway/Heavy Rail) • Accessibility: 100% Universal. All stations have elevators. • The Blue Line (East-West Spine): • Connects Narraganset Esplanade (BSU) to Beacon Central (Downtown) and the western suburbs. • The Red Line (The "J-Loop"): • Route: Starts North at Regalis Park (base of King's College hill), runs South along the coast to BSU, turns inland to Downtown, then hooks Southwest to the Airport. • Speed: Slow/Local. 55 minutes from BSU to Airport. • Function: Neighborhood hopper. B. The Regional Rail (The Express) • Line: The South Shore Line. • Equipment: BiLevel Coaches with "Low Floor" boarding (roll-on/roll-off). • Route: A diagonal "Hypotenuse" express track. • Stops: Iron Point (Home) --> BCI Airport --> Beacon Central --> Narraganset Esplanade (BSU). • Speed: 22 minutes from Airport to Campus. • Function: The "Teleporter." C. The Bus Network • The Core Network: Key routes in the city center run 24 Hours a Day, 7 Days a Week. High frequency (every 10-15 mins). • Route 99 (The Scrub Bus): The only bus that goes near King’s College. It stops outside the gate. Used exclusively by service staff. D. The Hub: Narraganset Esplanade Station Located on the BSU campus. • Level 2 (Elevated): Regional Rail (South Shore Line). Beautiful view of Narraganset Bay and the Newport Bridge. • Level 1 (Street Level Plaza): Bus Terminal & The Esplanade (Car-free zone). • Level B-M (Underground 1): Blue Line Metro. • Level B-R (Underground 2): Red Line Metro. Braxton Transit Authority (BTA) Official Tariff & Fare Guide Effective Date: July 1, 2006

  4. Fare Categories • Adult Fare: Standard pricing for riders ages 18–64. • Reduced Fare: Available to Seniors (65+), Medicare Card Holders, Persons with Disabilities, and Youth (5–17). • Calculation Rule: 50% of Adult Fare, rounded DOWN to the nearest $0.25. • ID Requirement: Proof of age or BTA Reduced Fare ID required upon request. • Student Fare: FREE. Valid K-12 Public School ID or Braxton State University ID required.

  8. Transfer Policy (The "Smart" Rules) The Window: Transfers are valid for 120 Minutes (2 Hours) from initial Tap On. Rule A: Lateral Transfer (Same or Lower Price) • Example: Bus ($2.00) --> Bus ($2.00). • Cost: Free. • Time: The 2-hour clock continues counting down. It does not reset. Rule B: Step-Up Transfer (Higher Price) • Example: Bus ($2.00) --> Metro ($2.75). • Cost: Pay the difference ($0.75). • Time: The 2-hour clock RESETS to zero. • Why: Because you paid "New Money" into the system, you get "New Time." Rule C: The "Ghost" Penalty (Rail Only) • You must Tap On and Tap Off for Regional Rail. • Failure to Tap Off: The system assumes you rode to Zone 15. • Penalty Charge: $30.00 (Maximum Peak Fare) deducted from LinkPass balance.

BTA / BSU Policy: The "Bull Pass" Identity

  1. What is it? • Official Name: The Braxton State University Identification Card. • Colloquial Name: "The Bull Pass." • Function: It is a 4-in-1 Smart Card. • Building Key: Access to dorms, labs, and practice rooms. • Meal Plan: Swipe for dining halls. • Library Card: Check out materials. • Transit Pass: Hard-coded as a Zone 15 Unlimited LinkPass.

  2. Activation • Status: Instant. • The Process: The moment the card is printed at the ID Office and handed to the student/staff member, the RFID chip is live. You can walk out of the office, walk onto a bus, and it works.

  3. The "Lost Card" Penalty Because the card is worth roughly $600/month in transit value alone, losing it is a crisis. • Replacement Fee: $25.00 (University Fee). • The Real Cost: You have to pay standard BTA fares (cash or vanilla LinkPass) until you get the new one.

  4. Key Locations

Braxton International Airport (BCI) • Location: 25 miles Southwest of the city. • Transit: Served by both the Red Line (Local/Underground) and South Shore Line (Express/Elevated). • Features: The "Transfer Core"—glass elevators connecting the two rail systems. Location Profile: Narraganset Esplanade Station

  1. Overview • Official Name: Narraganset Esplanade Transit Center. • Rank: #2 Busiest Station in the BTA System (behind Braxton Central). • Nickname: "The Lantern" or "The Point." • Location: The eastern tip of the Braxton Point Peninsula, marking the boundary between the Braxton State University (BSU) campus and Narraganset Bay. • Architectural Style: Marine Modernism. • Design: Soaring white tensile fabric roofs (evoking sails) and floor-to-ceiling glass walls facing the Narraganset. At night, it glows like a beacon visible from ships entering the harbor. • Significance: It is the primary interchange for the Eastern Zone of the metropolis. It doesn't just serve students; it serves tourists, festival-goers, and commuters transferring from the ferries.
  2. The Layout: "The Stack" The station is a masterclass in density, built into the slope of the reclaimed parkland to allow seamless transitions between levels. Level 2: The Sky Platform (Regional Rail Terminus) • Structure: An elevated viaduct running parallel to the coastline. • Service: • The South Shore Line: (The Fortress Commute). Terminates here. • The North Coast Line: Terminates here. • Capacity: 4 Tracks. It handles the volume of a major city terminal. • The Experience: Waiting for the train means staring out at the open Narraganset. Leo loves this platform because on stormy days, he can watch the waves crash against the sea wall from the safety of the heated glass shelter. • Accessibility: 100% Level Boarding. The platform is raised to match the floor height of the BiLevel coaches perfectly. Level 1: The Plaza & Bus Terminal (Street Level) • The Hub: A semi-circular, covered bus port with 12 Bays. • Service: • BTA City Bus: Eastern routes connecting the beach neighborhoods. • The Bull Run: The BSU campus circulator shuttles (Routes 80, 81, 82). • The "No Car" Zone: The plaza opens directly onto the Esplanade. No private cars are allowed within 500 feet of the station entrance. It is a pedestrian fortress. Level C: The Concourse (Underground) • Function: The ticket hall, retail promenade, and BSU Welcome Center. • The Feature: "The Reclamation Wall." A massive mural depicting the history of the site—from the toxic fires of the Braxton Steel Works to the green park of today. • The Meeting Point: "The Bull Pen." A bronze statue of the BSU Bull near the fare gates where the Fortress meets up. Level M: The Deep (Metro Terminus, also underground) • Service: • (Level M-B, upper level Metro) Blue Line: Terminates here (East-West Spine to Downtown). • (Level M-RS, lower level Metro) Red Line / Silver Line: Through-service (North-South). • Design: Features a massive "light well"—a glass skylight in the plaza above that lets natural daylight filter down 60 feet to the subway platform.
  3. The Ferry Dock: "The Sea Gate" Integrated directly into the station’s sea wall is the BTA Harbor Ferry Terminal. • Routes: • Navy Yard Shuttle: Connects BSU Engineering students to internships at the shipyard across the river. • Portside Connector: Connects to the industrial district. • Newport Flyer: Connects to Perotti Park in Newport, RI • The Vibe: It smells of diesel and salt water. The gangways are hydraulic and perfectly level (to the best of the BTA’s ability given we are on the Narraganset Bay).
  4. Audio Script: The "End of the Line" Because this is a major terminus, the announcements carry a sense of finality and transition. Context: On board the South Shore Line as it glides into the elevated station. The bay fills the windows. The Announcement: (Ding-Dong) "Now arriving at... Narraganset Esplanade. "This is the final stop for this train. Please check around for personal belongings. "Transfer here for Blue, Red, and Silver Metro lines. "Connection to BTA Local Bus and Harbor Ferry services on the Plaza Level. "Welcome to Braxton State University and the Narraganset Esplanade. "Please exit to the Right toward the bay." Location Profile: Braxton Central Terminal (BCT)
  5. Overview • Official Name: The Braxton Central Intermodal Terminal. • Nickname: "The Glass Cathedral," "The Central," or simply "The Hub." • Opened: October 15, 2005. (It still smells like new steel and ozone when the family arrives in 2006). • Location: Downtown Braxton, bridging the gap between the Financial District and the Historic Riverfront. • Architectural Style: High-Tech Modernism. • Inspiration: Berlin Hauptbahnhof (Berlin Central Station). • The Design: A massive, transparent exoskeleton of curved glass and tubular steel. There are no dark corners. Natural light filters from the roof all the way down to the subway platforms 60 feet below ground. • The Philosophy: "Visible Motion." From the central atrium, you can see trains moving East-West in the sky, and trains moving North-South in the earth.
